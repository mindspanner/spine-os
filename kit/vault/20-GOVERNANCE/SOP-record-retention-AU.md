# SOP — Record retention (keep what you must, bin what you should)

- **When to run this:** set the rules once, then review during a yearly clean-up. Apply the "destroy" half whenever a retention period clearly ends.
- **Owner:** you
- **Last reviewed:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Purpose

Decide, in advance, how long you keep each kind of record and what you do when that time is up. Keeping everything forever is a privacy and security liability; binning things too early can breach obligations you didn't know you had. This SOP gives you a place to write your decisions down — it does **not** state the legal retention periods for you, because those depend on your industry, structure and the records involved.

## Inputs

- The kinds of records your business creates (financial, client, contracts, marketing, HR if you have staff).
- The retention requirements that actually apply to *you* — from the ATO, your professional body, your insurer, any industry regulator, and the Australian Privacy Principles where personal information is involved.

## Outputs

- A simple retention table in this file: record type → how long → why → what happens at the end.
- A repeatable, recorded destruction step for anything past its period.

## How to build your retention table

For each record type, fill four columns:

| Record type | Keep for | Basis (who requires it) | At end of period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial / tax records | *look it up* | ATO guidance for your structure | Securely destroy or archive |
| Client / matter files | *look it up* | Your profession + privacy duties | Destroy or de-identify |
| Contracts & agreements | *look it up* | Limitation periods, your insurer | Archive then destroy |
| Marketing / consent records | *look it up* | Privacy / spam rules | Destroy when consent lapses |
| Incident records | *look it up* | Your own + any regulator | Archive |

> Replace every "*look it up*" with the actual period **you** confirmed. The ATO publishes general
> guidance on how long to keep tax records; your professional body and insurer may require longer for
> client matters. Confirm before you rely on a number.

## The two key principles (these are general, the periods are not)

1. **Keep what you must.** Some records have a minimum keep-period set by law or your profession. Find yours and write them above.
2. **Don't keep what you needn't.** Under the Australian Privacy Principles, personal information you no longer need (and aren't required to keep) should be destroyed or de-identified. Holding old personal data "just in case" is itself a risk.

## Steps

1. **List your record types** (use the table above as a starting point).
2. **Find each period** from the authoritative source for that record. Don't guess; cite where you got it.
3. **Write it in the table** with the basis and the end-of-period action.
4. **Destroy securely when the time comes.** Shred paper; securely delete digital files (empty trash, and remember backups also hold copies — your retention applies to backups too).
5. **Record the destruction.** A one-line log: date · what was destroyed · which rule said it could be. This protects you as much as keeping things does.
6. **Review yearly.** Periods and obligations change; revisit the table once a year.

## Done when

Every record type your business creates has a written retention period with a source, an end-of-period action, and a destruction step you can repeat and log.

## Worked example (illustrative periods — confirm your own)

A sole-trader's filled table fragment, after she did her homework:

- *Financial/tax records* — "Keep [period she confirmed from ATO guidance] · Basis: ATO · At end: securely delete after exporting an archive PDF."
- *Client files* — "Keep [period she confirmed with her professional association] · Basis: assoc. + privacy · At end: de-identify, then delete."
- She logs each clean-out: `2026-06-30 · destroyed 2018 receipts past retention · per ATO record-keeping period`.

> This SOP is a framework for recording your own decisions. It is not legal, tax or compliance advice
> and does not state any retention period for you. Confirm periods with the ATO, your professional
> body, your insurer and current privacy guidance, and seek advice where the answer matters.
