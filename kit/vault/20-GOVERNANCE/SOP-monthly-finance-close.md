# SOP — Monthly finance close (so the books are never a surprise)

- **When to run this:** the first few working days of each month, for the month just finished.
- **Owner:** you
- **Last reviewed:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Purpose

Pull the previous month to a clean stop: every invoice sent, every bill recorded, every dollar in and out accounted for, and the GST set-aside known — so quarterly BAS and end-of-year tax are a printout, not an archaeology dig. This SOP is about *cadence and completeness*, not bookkeeping technique, and it is not financial or tax advice.

## Inputs

- Last month's bank and card statements (or your accounting tool's feed).
- Invoices you issued and bills/receipts you received.
- Your accounting software or spreadsheet of record.

## Outputs

- Reconciled month: bank balance matches your records.
- All invoices issued and chased; overdue list known.
- GST collected vs paid noted, and the GST portion set aside (if you're registered).
- A one-line "month closed" note in `30-CLIENTS/` or a finance note, dated.

## Steps

1. **Issue anything outstanding.** Send every invoice for work delivered last month. You can't close a month you haven't billed.
2. **Gather expenses.** Collect receipts and bills. Anything missing a receipt, chase now while you still remember what it was.
3. **Reconcile.** Match every line on the bank statement to an invoice, a bill, or a transfer. Unmatched lines are the whole point — they're where errors and forgotten income hide.
4. **Chase the overdue.** List invoices past their terms. Send a polite reminder on each. Note any you expect to fight for.
5. **Set aside tax/GST.** If you're GST-registered, work out GST collected minus GST paid and move that amount to a separate account or, at minimum, write down what's owed so you don't spend it. Do the same for income-tax provisioning if that's your habit.
6. **Snapshot the numbers.** Note month's income, expenses, and rough profit somewhere stable. Three numbers, once a month, is a trend you can act on.
7. **Close it.** Write a dated "<Month> closed" line. Don't reopen a closed month except to correct a real error (and note the correction).

## Done when

The bank reconciles to your records, every delivered job is invoiced, the overdue list is current, the tax/GST set-aside is done, and the month is marked closed with a date.

## Worked example

A sole-trader plumber closing March:

1. Issues the two job invoices he'd let sit.
2. Finds all receipts except one ($95 hardware) — texts the supplier for a copy.
3. Reconciles: every bank line matches except a $220 deposit he'd forgotten was a returned customer overpayment — fixes it.
4. Two invoices overdue; sends reminders.
5. GST: collected $1,940, paid $610 → sets aside $1,330.
6. Snapshot: income $19,400 · expenses $7,100 · ~$12,300 before tax.
7. Writes `March closed 2026-04-03`.
